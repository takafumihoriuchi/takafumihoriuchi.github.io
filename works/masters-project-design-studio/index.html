Overview

During the Design & Engineering master's programme at Politecnico di Milano, I joined a different team each semester in the Design Studio courses. We developed each product from the problem and user profile through form, mechanism, interaction, and manufacturing. This page brings together three projects completed from winter 2019 to winter 2020.

  1. Coffee-machine design (winter 2019)HUBBLE adapts one shared machine to different tastes through a circular interface, movable nozzle, rotating dispensing head, internal engineering, and production drawings.
  2. PAPR design for welders (summer 2020)VCS-20 works with an existing welding shield, combining a hood, vest, and shoulder-mounted blower in a wearable prototype.
  3. Blood-donation screening-device design (winter 2020)DELCON Massachusetts combines contactless temperature, heart-rate, blood-pressure, and haemoglobin screening with a self-service QR and cloud workflow.

Coffee-machine design (winter 2019)

Problem — Sharing one machine without giving everyone the same coffee

In shared flats, coworking spaces, and offices, one machine serves people with different cultures and preferences. Existing capsule machines offered limited cup sizes, unclear controls, and flavour tied to the capsule. Repeating a detailed setup for every drink would also make a shared appliance cumbersome. We therefore contrasted Maria, who prefers a large mild coffee, with Mert, who prefers a small strong one, and set requirements for adjustable aroma, strength, temperature, and volume; remembered users and recipes; varied cups and orientations; and straightforward refilling and cleaning.

What we designed — HUBBLE remembers the person and concentrates control in one circle

HUBBLE uses Bluetooth to recognise a user and retrieve saved recipes. On first use, the circular knob is turned to choose aroma, strength, temperature, and other settings, then pressed to confirm. A preferred drink can be named and saved, while recommendations invite a different choice. Combining display, rotation, and push in one control avoids a crowded front panel. The nozzle moves vertically to keep an appropriate distance from anything from an espresso cup to a tall mug.

One structure for placement, refilling, and maintenance

The dispensing head rotates so a cup can approach from either the long or short side of the counter. Water and bean tanks lift out from above, while the milk container and waste bin push open from the front. The enclosure also accommodates a grinder, pump, heater, brewing mechanism, and rotation motor. We developed the internal layout, 3D CAD, assembly and parts list, and manufacturing drawings for the individual components.

Outcome — Connecting appearance, interaction, and engineering at drawing level

The outcome integrates scenarios and personas, product styling, circular-interface flows, moving and service parts, internal layout, 3D CAD, assembly drawings, and part drawings. It reframes a shared appliance from a machine that gives everyone the same cup into one that remembers each person's preferences. The source material does not confirm a physical machine or extraction-performance testing, so the result is presented as a design developed through CAD and drawings.

Product Development Design Studio I, Coffee Machine, Group 7. Joint work by Carla Ayala, Fabio Monza, Ayse Selin Yagsan, Takafumi Horiuchi, and Marco Talpo.

PAPR design for welders (summer 2020)

Problem — Protecting breathing while retaining an existing welding shield

Welders need protection from fumes and hot sparks, yet layered equipment in confined workplaces can complicate dressing and obstruct vision and movement. The brief was therefore a light, quick-to-don powered air-purifying respirator that could be added to an existing welding shield. The persona Antonio informed requirements including fit with different shields, compactness, comfort, a replaceable long-life battery, positive pressure, at least two airflow settings, visible and audible alarms, a combined filter, and airflow above 120 L/min.

What we designed — VCS-20 integrates the hood, vest, and shoulder unit

VCS-20 combines a universal hood, a vest, and a C-shaped PAPR unit. Stretch panels and six sections of Gecko Tape adapt the flame-resistant cotton hood to different shields, and the shield alone can be lifted during a pause. The vest stabilises the system; a magnetic neck connector supplies air to the hood; and articulated rear and side sections distribute the blower's weight across both shoulders instead of hanging it from the neck.

Controls and feedback that remain usable with gloves

A push-and-rotate controller combines power and airflow adjustment in one place. Visual and audible feedback communicates filter and battery condition and reduced airflow. The proposal also considers injection-moulded shells, laser-cut textile parts, material selection, part division, and assembly. To examine fit and dressing, the team built a full-scale wearable appearance prototype.

Outcome — Testing how the system sits on the body

The deliverables cover user requirements, the wearing system, external and internal configuration, materials and manufacturing, and a full-scale wearable prototype. They treat the PAPR not as a stand-alone blower but as one wearable product including shield and clothing. The source contains no measured results for airflow, positive pressure, noise, battery life, filtration, safety, or usability with welders; the outcome is therefore a developed prototype rather than a performance-validated product.

Product Development Studio 2, Group 8. Joint work by Takafumi Horiuchi, Hou Weihuan, Giulia Stevanoni, and Marco Luigi Talpo.

Blood-donation screening-device design (winter 2020)

Problem — Letting donors complete pre-donation measurements without contact

Before donation, temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, and haemoglobin are checked. During COVID-19, shared equipment and close staff contact added hygiene concerns and stress. We proposed a self-service unit for donors aged 16 and over in North America, for use in donation centres, mobile venues, and buses. The main requirements were contactless and non-invasive measurement, an easy-to-clean portable form, short measurement time, paperless records, secure data sharing, and both tabletop and standing use.

What we designed — Four indicators through three optical methods

The concept combines iPPG for estimating heart rate and blood pressure from facial colour changes, thermography for surface temperature, and a camera plus machine learning for estimating haemoglobin from the nail bed. These methods were selected to avoid attaching sensors to the body or taking blood.

Methods and assumed values stated in the source material; the figures are not validation results.

From a QR code to shared results

The donor scans the QR code received at preregistration, presents a nail to the upper machine-vision camera for haemoglobin estimation, then stands in front of the same camera and the thermal camera for temperature, heart rate, and blood pressure. The screen and speaker guide position and progress; results are added to the cloud record for staff review.

One unit for a tabletop or a floor stand

The same unit can sit directly on a table or attach to a dedicated stand with two pin-selected heights. Cameras and supporting lights are grouped at the top, with screen and speaker on the front, and power by battery or cable. Candidate production methods include injection-moulded PC and PET front parts, a thermoformed and machined rear, a polycarbonate screen cover, and an A500 steel stand. Internal layout, assembly, manufacturing, and stand stability were developed, including finite-element analysis.

Outcome — Bringing contactless measurement and staff workflow into one unit

The concept design brings together use settings and requirements, measurement methods, a QR and cloud workflow, tabletop and floor-standing enclosures, internal layout, materials and manufacturing, exploded views, and structural analysis. Rather than placing separate instruments side by side, it connects the donor's sequence and the staff review as one service.

Design Studio, Group 11. Joint work by Fabio Villano, Bhuvanesh Bari, Takafumi Horiuchi, and Joeperdo El Husseini.
