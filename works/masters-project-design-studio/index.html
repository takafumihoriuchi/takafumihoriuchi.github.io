Overview

As part of the Design & Engineering master's program at Politecnico di Milano, I worked with a different team each semester in the Design Studio courses. We developed integrated product proposals, from defining the problem and user needs to designing the form, mechanisms, interactions, and manufacturing approach. This page presents three projects completed between winter 2019 and winter 2020.

  1. Coffee machine design (winter 2019)HUBBLE is a shared coffee machine concept designed around individual preferences. The proposal includes a circular interface, a height-adjustable nozzle, a rotating dispensing head, internal mechanisms, and manufacturing drawings.
  2. PAPR design for welders (summer 2020)VCS-20 is a powered air-purifying respirator concept designed to work with existing welding shields. It combines a hood, vest, and shoulder-mounted blower, developed into a full-scale wearable prototype.
  3. Blood donor screening device design (winter 2020)DELCON Massachusetts is a concept for contactless pre-donation screening of temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, and hemoglobin. We designed the enclosure and a self-service experience covering QR check-in, measurement, and cloud-based sharing of results.

Coffee machine design (winter 2019)

Challenge: Personalized coffee from a shared machine

In shared flats, coworking spaces, and offices, one coffee machine serves people with different cultural backgrounds and preferences. Our review of existing capsule machines identified limits on cup size, unclear controls, and flavors determined by the capsule. Requiring people to repeat detailed settings for every drink would also make a shared machine cumbersome.

We developed two contrasting personas: Maria, from Mexico, who prefers a large, mild coffee, and Mert, from Turkey, who prefers a small, strong one. These personas informed requirements for adjustable aroma, strength, temperature, and volume; saved user profiles and recipes; support for different cup sizes and installation orientations; and easy refilling and cleaning.

Design: Saved preferences and a circular interface

HUBBLE was designed to recognize users via Bluetooth and retrieve their saved recipes. On first use, the user turns the circular knob to select aroma, strength, temperature, and other settings, then presses it to confirm. A favorite drink can be named and saved for quick selection next time, while recommendations offer a way to try other flavors.

The interface combines a circular display with a rotary push control, keeping the front panel uncluttered and showing the setting sequence and current values in one place. The nozzle moves vertically to suit the cup, maintaining an appropriate distance from a small espresso cup or a tall mug.

Integrating placement, refilling, and maintenance

The dispensing head rotates relative to the body so that cups can be placed along either the long or short side of the counter. This lets the machine adapt to walls and circulation routes in compact kitchens and offices. The water and bean tanks lift out from the top, while the milk container and waste bin open with a push from the front, providing access for refilling and cleaning from more than one direction.

We also arranged the grinder, pump, heater, brewing mechanism, and rotation motor within the enclosure and developed the 3D CAD model and component structure. The final drawing set includes assembly drawings, a bill of materials, and manufacturing drawings for individual parts.

Outcome: An integrated design developed through CAD and engineering drawings

The final proposal integrates requirements based on use scenarios and personas, product styling, circular interface flows, moving components, maintenance access, internal layout, 3D CAD, assembly drawings, and part drawings. It addresses shared use through a machine designed to remember each person's preferences.

Product Development Design Studio I, Coffee Machine, Group 7. Joint work by Carla Ayala, Fabio Monza, Ayse Selin Yagsan, Takafumi Horiuchi, and Marco Talpo.

PAPR design for welders (summer 2020)

Challenge: Respiratory protection that works with existing welding shields

Welders need respiratory and facial protection from fumes and hot sparks. In confined workspaces, however, layers of protective equipment can make it difficult to put on and remove gear and can obstruct vision and movement. The brief called for a lightweight powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR) that could be fitted quickly to an existing welding shield.

We created Antonio, a 25-year-old full-time welder in Milan, as the project persona. The design requirements included compatibility with different shields, a compact form, comfort, a replaceable battery with a long operating life, positive pressure, at least two airflow settings, visual and audible alarms, a combined filter, and airflow of at least 120 L/min.

Design: A hood, vest, and shoulder-supported PAPR unit

VCS-20 combines an adaptable hood, a vest, and a C-shaped PAPR unit. The hood pairs flame-resistant cotton with stretch fabric and uses six Gecko Tape attachment points to fit different shield shapes. The shield can be lifted on its own during a break, without removing the entire system.

The vest limits movement of the unit and adjusts to the wearer with hook-and-loop fasteners and straps. A magnetic connector at the neck supplies air to the hood, while the blower unit distributes its weight across both shoulders. Articulated rear and side joints and silicone components were designed to accommodate different body sizes and movement.

Designing controls and feedback for use with gloves

A push-and-rotate controller brings power and airflow adjustment into one control. The proposed visual and audible feedback indicates filter condition, battery status, and reduced airflow. We considered injection-molded shells and laser-cut textile parts, developing the material choices, component divisions, and assembly approach.

The team built a full-scale, wearable appearance prototype to explore fit and the process of putting on and removing the system.

Outcome: Exploring fit through prototyping and simulating operation

The project brought together user requirements, the wearing system, exterior and internal layouts, materials and manufacturing methods, and a full-scale wearable prototype. It developed the PAPR as part of an integrated wearable system that includes the shield and clothing.

Product Development Studio 2, Group 8. Joint work by Takafumi Horiuchi, Hou Weihuan, Giulia Stevanoni, and Marco Luigi Talpo.

Blood donor screening device design (winter 2020)

Challenge: Self-service, contactless pre-donation screening

Pre-donation screening includes checks of temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, and hemoglobin. During the COVID-19 pandemic, shared equipment and close contact with staff raised additional hygiene concerns and stress. We reconsidered the measurement process from both perspectives.

The proposal was a self-service terminal for donors aged 16 and over in North America, intended for donation centers, mobile venues, and blood donation buses. The main requirements were contactless, non-invasive measurements; an easy-to-clean, portable form; short measurement times; paperless records; secure data sharing; and both tabletop and standing use.

Concept: Four screening indicators using three optical methods

The concept combines imaging photoplethysmography (iPPG) to estimate heart rate and blood pressure from facial color changes, thermography to read facial surface temperature, and camera images with machine learning to estimate hemoglobin from the nail bed. We selected these methods to explore measurement without attaching sensors to the body or taking blood samples.

Measurement methods and design assumptions documented in the project. These figures are not validated performance results. The definitions and sources of the ± values still need confirmation.
MeasurementMethodDesign assumptions in the project
Heart rate and blood pressureFacial iPPGDistance: 0.5–1 m; illuminance: 1000–4000 lx; measurement time: about 60 s. Heart rate: ±3 bpm; systolic blood pressure: ±0.4 mmHg; diastolic blood pressure: ±0.2 mmHg.
TemperatureThermographyDistance: 0.3–1 m; real-time measurement; ±0.2 °C.
HemoglobinNail-bed images and machine learningIlluminance: 250–3000 lx; measurement time: about 15 s; ±0.92 g/dL.

From a QR code to shared results

In the proposed workflow, the donor scans the QR code received during preregistration, then presents a fingernail to the upper machine-vision camera for hemoglobin estimation. Next, the donor stands in front of the terminal so the same camera and the thermal camera can capture the images needed for temperature, heart rate, and blood pressure screening. The screen and speaker would guide positioning and progress, and the results would be added to a cloud record for staff review.

Adapting one terminal for tabletop or floor-standing use

We designed the same terminal for direct placement on a table or attachment to a dedicated floor stand, with a pin mechanism offering two height settings. The machine-vision camera, thermal camera, and supporting lights are grouped at the top, with the screen and speaker on the front. The design allows for battery or cable power.

Proposed materials and processes include injection-molded PC and PET front components, a thermoformed and machined rear enclosure, a polycarbonate screen cover, and an A500 steel stand. We developed the internal layout, exploded views, assembly sequence, and manufacturing approach, and examined the stand’s structural stability using finite-element analysis.

Outcome: An integrated screening concept and staff workflow

The final concept brings together use settings and requirements, selected measurement methods, a QR and cloud-based workflow, tabletop and floor-standing enclosures, internal layout, materials and manufacturing methods, exploded views, and structural analysis.

Design Studio, Group 11. Joint work by Fabio Villano, Bhuvanesh Bari, Takafumi Horiuchi, and Joeperdo El Husseini.
